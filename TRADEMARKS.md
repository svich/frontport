Version 1.0 · Effective date: 2026-10-24

## Code rights and brand rights are separate

[GNU General Public License v3.0](LICENSE). The GPL lets anyone copy, modify,
distribute and sell Frontport, on its terms. This policy does not restrict,
override or add conditions to any of those permissions. See the GPL FAQ on
[selling copies](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#DoesTheGPLAllowMoney).

**The brand** is not licensed by the GPL. GPL-3.0
[section 7(e)](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-3.0.html#section7) lets a
licensor decline to grant rights under trademark law. This policy explains how
the Frontport name and branding may be used, so that people can tell official
Frontport releases apart from unofficial ones.

This policy applies only to the extent the trademark owner holds rights under
applicable law. Nothing in it limits uses that the law allows without
permission, such as fair use or truthful references to Frontport.

## Protected identifiers

This policy covers:

- the name **Frontport**;
- the Frontport logo;
- the official application icons; and
- other official branding assets published by the trademark owner for

Together these are the "Marks". The policy also covers names and logos
confusingly similar to the Marks.

## Uses that don't need permission

You may, without asking:

- **Refer to the official project** by name.
- **Link to the official repository** at
  <https://github.com/svich/frontport>.
- **Describe compatibility**, for example "works with Frontport" or
  "compatible with Frontport".
- **Make academic, educational and editorial references**, such as research,
  teaching material, articles, reviews, tutorials, news and videos.
- **Hold clearly identified unofficial community discussions**, such as
  forums, chat groups, wikis and guides, as long as they say they are
  unofficial.
- **Say that your fork is based on Frontport**, for example "based on
  Frontport" or "a fork of Frontport".
- **Redistribute unmodified official releases**, with or without charging a
  fee, as long as you don't suggest that the trademark owner endorses you or
  your distribution.

When you do, use the name in plain text rather than the logo, don't make it
more prominent than your own name, and don't suggest that the trademark owner
sponsors or endorses you.

## Uses that need permission

You need written permission from the trademark owner to:

- distribute a modified application under the Frontport name;
- use the official logo or icons for a modified build;
- present an unofficial fork as an official Frontport release; or
- use the Marks in commercial products, services or merchandise in a way that
  suggests they are authorized by the trademark owner or the Frontport
  project.

## Forks

You may fork Frontport, and distribute or sell your fork, under GPL-3.0
without asking. To the extent trademark law allows the trademark owner to
require it, a modified version that you distribute publicly must:

1. use a name that is not "Frontport" and is not confusingly similar to it;
2. use its own logo and icons instead of the official ones; and
3. not present itself as an official Frontport release.

We also ask, without requiring it, that forks:

- use their own bundle identifier and code-signing identity, so macOS and
  users can tell the apps apart;
- point update checks and other online services at their own
  infrastructure; and
- say in their README and About window that they are unofficial and based on
  Frontport.

Keep the copyright notices, license text and other legal notices that GPL-3.0
requires. Keeping them, and saying your work is based on Frontport, is not a
trademark violation.

You don't need to rename identifiers users don't see, such as source file
names, Swift module names or type names. Builds you don't distribute publicly,
for personal use, testing or internal use in your organization, don't need to
be renamed.
